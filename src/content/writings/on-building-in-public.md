## The Fear of Imperfection

Most people don't publish until something is polished. I used to be the same way. I'd work on projects for months, keep them private, and then either abandon them or release them with a polished README that hid all the struggle.

That approach has two problems:

1. **You lose the narrative.** The journey is more interesting than the destination. The decisions, the wrong turns, the "aha" moments — these are what other builders learn from.

2. **You build alone.** When nobody knows what you're working on, nobody can help, challenge, or collaborate.

## What "Building in Public" Actually Means

It's not about posting daily updates on Twitter. It's about making your work visible at every stage:

- **The messy prototype** — share the first version that barely works
- **The failed experiment** — document what didn't work and why
- **The redesign** — explain why the old approach failed
- **The dead end** — mark it clearly, don't hide it

## This Site Is the Manifesto

Every project here has a journal. Every journal has failures. Every failure has a lesson.

If you only see successes, you're not seeing the real work.
